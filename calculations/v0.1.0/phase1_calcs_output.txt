(50 E24 pairs satisfy both limits; best-margin pairs shown. Margins are thin: window is 0.600 V wide.)

Sensitivity of example 2.0k/3.0k (ratio 0.600) to the UNVERIFIED Echo level:
  Echo HIGH = 5.25 V -> GPIO 3.150 V : OK; OK@3.3
  Echo HIGH = 5.00 V -> GPIO 3.000 V : OK; OK@3.3
  Echo HIGH = 4.75 V -> GPIO 2.850 V : OK; OK@3.3
  Echo HIGH = 4.50 V -> GPIO 2.700 V : TOO LOW (no valid HIGH at VDD=3.6); OK@3.3
  Echo HIGH = 4.00 V -> GPIO 2.400 V : TOO LOW (no valid HIGH at VDD=3.6); fails@3.3
  Echo HIGH = 3.30 V -> GPIO 1.980 V : TOO LOW (no valid HIGH at VDD=3.6); fails@3.3
Conclusion: a passive divider only works if Echo HIGH is close to 5 V. A 3.3 V-output ('modern') module
powered from 3.3 V must NOT get a divider. Echo level must be measured on the chosen module.
Divider RC (Thevenin 1200 ohm x 12 pF) = 14.4 ns -> negligible vs echo pulse widths (us-ms).

3. ESP32 -> HC-SR04 Trigger
==============================================================================
ESP32 VOH(min) at 3.3 V = 2.64 V (VERIFIED formula).
  vs TTL-type input, VIH=2.0 V (illustrative): meets threshold
  vs CMOS input at 5 V, VIH=0.7*VCC (illustrative): DOES NOT meet threshold
The sensor's actual trigger threshold is UNVERIFIED (no manufacturer datasheet): Trigger compatibility is TBD.

==============================================================================

  Standard-mode (100 kHz)  Cb=  50 pF -> Rp(max) = tr/(0.8473*Cb) =    23604 ohm

pull-ups; the parallel combination must stay >= Rp(min). ESP32 internal pull-ups (45 kohm typ.) are too weak.
